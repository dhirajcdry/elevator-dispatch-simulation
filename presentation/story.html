Rules

  • Each passenger gets an elevator as soon as they request.
  • Once assigned, the elevator can’t change.

Elevator assignment · method 1

Round robin

Assign elevators in turn: A, B, A, B, and so on.

+Pros

  • Nothing to work outIt needs no information about the elevators, so every decision is instant.
  • Shares a rush evenlyIf ten passengers request at the lobby at once, elevators A and B are each assigned five.

−Cons

  • Ignores where elevators areHere it assigns Chris to elevator A, which must reach 51 first: 90 ticks instead of 62.
  • Ignores how busy they areA full elevator still gets its turn.

Elevator assignment · method 2

Nearest elevator

Assign the elevator that can reach the passenger in the fewest floors, counting any trip it must finish first.

+Pros

  • Uses position and directionIt counts how far each elevator must go before it can reach you. Here it assigns Chris to elevator B: 62 ticks, not 90.

−Cons

  • Counts floors, not roomIt cannot tell whether the elevator will have space when it reaches you. A full elevator going your way still looks nearest.

Decision 2

Service order

Which waiting passenger does the elevator pick up next?

Decidedwhen a passenger joins the elevator. Until then, it follows its plan. Matterswhen two or more passengers are waiting for the same elevator. Controlswhen each passenger is picked up, so only the service order can make someone wait forever.

Service order · method 1

Request order

Pick up passengers in the order they requested.

+Pros

  • First come, first servedAlice requested first, so she is picked up first. No passenger who requested later goes ahead of her.
  • No passenger waits foreverYour wait depends only on the passengers who requested before you.

−Cons

  • Drives past passengersBob, at floor 5, waits while the elevator serves Alice at 30: 61 ticks instead of 9.

Next

Request order drives past passengers it could pick up. Direction-based picks up anyone heading its way as it passes.

1 elevator, starting at floor 1 · floors 1–31

Service order · method 2

Direction-based

On the way, pick up every passenger going the same direction who fits. Turn around only when empty, with nobody waiting ahead.

+Pros

  • FamiliarMost elevators already work this way, stopping on the way for passengers going the same direction.
  • Pickups on the way are freeBob is picked up on the way up to Alice: 9 ticks instead of 61, and Alice loses nothing.

−Cons

  • Will not turn back earlyBob, going down from 5, waits for Alice's whole trip: 59 ticks. Picking him up first gives 7 and costs Alice 6.
  • Not first come, first servedA later passenger can go first. If the elevator always reaches someone full, their wait has no limit.

Next

Direction-based never checks whether another order would get everyone there sooner, and nothing limits how long a passenger can be passed. Forecast works out each order’s times, within a limit.

Allowed delay K

When Alice is assigned, forecast gives her an estimated drop-off time: 30. With K = 80, she may be dropped off at most 80 ticks later than that: by 110.

The rule

A newcomer goes first only if nobody waiting is pushed past their limit. At tick 79, the next short trip would push Alice to 112, so Alice goes first. She is dropped off at 108.

Cost

Forecast tries every position in an elevator's pickup order, so it slows down most when many passengers wait for the same elevator at once. Requests arrive one at a time, and even the slowest case takes about a tenth of a second per passenger.

How I checked it

  • 70 unit testsEvery rule and method on small examples worked out by hand, including the sample input.
  • Rules enforced in the codePicking up on the wrong floor, over capacity, in the wrong direction, or reversing with passengers aboard stops the run with an error.
  • Outside checks: 0 broken rules270 runs of my rule check, plus independent checks with their own traffic. Nothing broke, and every hand-worked case matched.
  • The allowed delay holdsNo passenger is dropped off more than 80 ticks after their first forecast.
  • No looking aheadRerunning with later requests removed gives the same position log up to that point.
  • Forecasts come trueWith no later requests, every first forecast equals the actual drop-off time.

What I would do with more time

Stop timeStop time is currently 0. In real buildings each stop costs time, which is where grouping passengers by destination helps.
Faster methodsEvery method here is a first version. Forecast repeats work; removing some made it 2 to 3 times faster in prototypes, same results.
Parking idle elevatorsMove idle elevators toward floors with the most recent requests.
Re-planning pickupsReorder the whole pickup order instead of only placing each new passenger.
Zones and express elevatorsBoth mean an elevator that serves only some floors: shorter trips in a rush, but it cannot help anywhere else.

Appendix · service_order.py · forecast_drop_off_times

Playing an elevator forward

Both forecasts use this. It copies one elevator and its part of the service order, then repeats four steps until everyone is dropped off.

0Copy; assign the newcomer and plan their pickupdeepcopy
1Board whoever the service order allowswho_boards
2Nobody aboard or waiting: return the drop-off ticksreturn
3Jump to the next floor where someone may get on or off, one tick per floornext_stop
4Drop off riders here and record the tickdrop_off

The same who_boards and next_target as the real run, so forecasts come true. Only the movement differs: no request arrives during a forecast, so it can jump between stops instead of moving one floor per tick.
